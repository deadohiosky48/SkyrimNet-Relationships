# Relationships

### Bonds that SkyrimNet characters earn with you

Everyone you get close to forms their own opinion of you — and it changes.

**[How it works, in pictures](https://deadohiosky48.github.io/SkyrimNet-Relationships/)**

## What this is

Relationships tracks the bond between you and the people of Skyrim across the
whole spectrum of human connection. Romance is one of the ways a bond can go,
not the only one and not the destination. A shield-sister who will never be a
lover and would die for you is a first-class outcome here, tracked with the same
care as a marriage. Two people who become inseparable without ever being in love
is a story this mod is built to tell.

It does not ask *how far up the ladder are we*. It asks *what kind of person is
this, what do they want, what will they refuse, and has any of that changed*.

## The principle everything follows from

**Describe objectively, let each NPC judge.**

There is no central "how good is the player" score — not for deeds, not for
looks. Every NPC receives the same objective facts and their own disposition
decides what those facts mean. The same act earns warmth from one person,
nothing from another, and contempt from a third, because they are different
people rather than the same person at different tiers.

That is why there are no archetypes. Each character is written from their own
bio by an LLM, once, and is theirs.

## What it does

**Enrolls the people who matter.** Anyone who stays with you as a follower for a
couple of game hours is enrolled on their own — even if you have told them to
wait somewhere. Anyone else, from the innkeeper you flirt with to a jarl whose
trust you have earned, you enroll yourself with a hotkey or from the dashboard.
Enrollment means "this person is being watched", not "something has started":
everyone begins as a Stranger with no points.

**Writes a character for each of them.** From their actual bio: what they are
drawn to, what closeness requires of them, how much of what they feel they show,
how singular they need a bond to be, one line explaining why, and one line they
will not cross whatever they feel.

**Two tracks, not one ladder.** Bond depth is earned by time together and is
open to everyone. The romantic track is separate, and getting onto it takes more
than time — see below.

**Scores conversation.** Talking matters. A background judge reads what actually
passed between you and decides whether it moved anything — usually it did not.
Anyone enrolled who is near you is judged, following you or not, and nothing is
lost if you walk away: the next time you are around them, everything since their
last look is read. Characters can also mark a moment themselves, mid-conversation,
when something you did or said genuinely changed how they feel. Pacing lives in
code, not in the prompt: daily caps, cooldowns, and a rarity limit on the moments
where two people redefine what they are.

**Lets people change.** Dispositions are reviewed as people live, and can move
in either direction — but only on a pattern of behavior across days, never on
one memorable evening, and only one step at a time. Someone who swore off
commitment can come to want it. Someone badly treated can close up.

**Tells you when something shifts, quietly.** When a bond deepens or cools you
get a notice — never in the middle of a fight, an OStim or SexLab scene, or a
pause; it waits until you can see it. The person themselves notices the shift
privately, as a thought of their own. Nobody nearby hears a narrator announce it.

**Keeps the record.** Every bond remembers what moved it and why, in that
person's own words, saved with your game so an older save has an older history.

## The gate between friendship and romance

This is the part that most distinguishes the mod, and there are two locks on it.

**Nothing becomes romantic on its own.** Spending a year together does not make
someone fall in love with you. A background assessor watches for a moment where
something between you actually crossed, and until it finds one, a person stays
platonic no matter how deep the bond runs. Someone can climb the whole ladder as
a friend and remain one. That is a real ending, not a failure state.

**And crossing takes both of you.** A spark is only their half of it. When
someone reaches the point of wanting more, they raise it themselves, in their
own words, and you answer plainly:

| your answer | what happens |
|---|---|
| you feel the same | the romantic track opens and the bond can keep climbing |
| you do not | they stay a friend, and the bond steps back to make room |
| say nothing for now | the question stays open; they will raise it again later |

**You can turn someone down.** Declining is a real answer with real weight. It
does not erase what they feel and it does not end the friendship, but the bond
does step back to the middle of Friend, because a refusal that left them one
good conversation short of asking again would be no refusal at all. It may sting
for a while, in whatever way that person shows hurt, and it eases as the
friendship grows again. **A no is an answer for now, not forever.** When they
climb back to where they were when they asked, they ask again, exactly as the
first time, and you can answer differently or the same.

**Nothing creeps past the question while it is unanswered.** The gate is on
romance, not on depth: someone who has sparked but whose question you have not
answered is held just short of Lover, so nobody becomes your lover by
accumulation alone. An award that would carry them across is held back rather
than paid and taken away, so you never see a tier arrive and vanish. Say yes and
it is granted in full. A purely platonic bond has no question to answer and
climbs to the top of the ladder freely.

**Wanting someone is not falling for them.** Desire never counts as a crossing:
not attraction, not a night together, however often. Intimacy itself follows each
person's disposition. A casual one can be your bedmate as a friend, with no
romance involved. A romantic one needs to have begun falling for you, though
nothing has to be declared yet. A guarded one waits for the deepest bond there
is. A recorded engagement or marriage counts as having fallen. And being free to
is not the same as wanting to: whether they want you tonight is its own question
each time, and yes one evening promises nothing about the next.

**Spouse is the rung that makes marriage possible, and it is enforced.** Until
someone has earned it, a formal proposal is off the table — MARAS will not
offer one, and an engagement that slips through some other route is put back. The
ceremony itself still happens through whatever marriage system you use; this mod
decides when you have both earned the right to hold it.

Reaching Spouse is ordinary progression, and talking about marriage is part of
it. Two people who are already lovers discussing a future, and meaning it, is one
of the largest things that can happen between them — so it moves the bond like
one, and it is often what carries them the last of the way.

**Getting the most out of it.** What this mod reads about the people you know,
and how to make sure your save has something worth reading — especially if you
are installing mid-playthrough with companions who already have history:
[Give It Something To Read](https://deadohiosky48.github.io/SkyrimNet-Relationships/preparing.html).

## The dashboard

Bind the **Dashboard Hotkey** (under *Dashboard* in the settings panel) and press
it anywhere. You see everyone you have a bond with: how deep it runs, whether
you are married or they are a marriage candidate, and the last thing that moved
each bond, right under their name. Open someone for their full card — who they
are, what they want, what they refuse, and the last several things that brought
you closer or pushed you apart.

If someone not yet enrolled was under your crosshair when you opened it, the
dashboard offers to **enroll** them. Each card also carries the repairs —
re-read their standing, re-author their character, correct one trait, remove
them from the roster — so none of them needs a key or the console.

**Developer view** (a setting, off by default) shows what characters have not
said aloud — feelings they have not spoken, a question they are waiting to ask,
points held back until you answer — and adds a few developer tools. The
dashboard is written to be played without it.

Press Escape to close it. The dashboard needs Meridian UI; without it everything
else works and only the dashboard is missing.

## Hotkeys

Four keys, all under SkyrimNet's plugin panel, all **unbound until you set
them**, each with an optional modifier (hold Left Shift with it, for example).
Changes take effect a couple of seconds after you save the settings — no reload.

| key | point at someone and press it to… |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard Hotkey** | open or close the dashboard |
| **Enroll Hotkey** | start a bond with them, following you or not |
| **Re-Read Hotkey** | read their standing again from the record as it now stands |
| **Re-Author Hotkey** | write their character again from scratch — no undo |

The three crosshair keys do nothing during an OStim or SexLab scene or a
conversation, where the crosshair rests on a partner or a speaker and scene
mods use the keyboard. The Re-Author Hotkey refuses a second press on the same
person within a minute.

A modifier is only checked by this mod: another mod bound to the same key still
fires, modifier or not. Pick keys nothing else in your load order uses.

## Installation

Install with a mod manager, or copy the contents over your `Data` folder.
`SNRom_Integration.esl` is ESL-flagged and does not consume a load-order slot.

Settings appear in SkyrimNet's plugin panel under **SkyrimNet Relationships**.

## Upgrading from 1.x

1.x kept each bond's points inside Romantasy. 2.0 keeps its own, and brings
yours over **once per save**, the first time that save loads with 2.0:

1. **Keep Romantasy installed for that first load.** Install 2.0 over 1.x, load
   your save, and wait for the notification that your companions were brought
   over. Nothing else needs doing; it runs in the background.
2. **Then Romantasy is optional.** Once a save has been brought over, 2.0 never
   reads Romantasy for it again. Keep it if you like it for its own sake — 2.0
   releases your companions from its scoring, so the two do not fight over them.
3. **Removing Romantasy entirely?** Saves made while it was installed still list
   its plugin, `CS_Romantasy.esp`, and may refuse to load without it. Install the
   optional **Romantasy Stub** download: an empty plugin with that name and
   nothing in it, which lets those saves load. **Never enable the stub alongside
   the real Romantasy**, and keep it enabled for as long as you play saves that
   needed it.

If a 1.x save is loaded with 2.0 and Romantasy already gone, nothing breaks:
everyone starts from zero and is read again from their history, as on a new game.

**What does not come over:** Romantasy's likes and dislikes. They were built on
game statistics ("Dungeons Cleared") rather than on who someone is, and 2.0
drops them entirely; preferences return in a later update, written in the mod's
own terms. Characters, histories, marriages and the answers you have given all
carry over unchanged.

**Your hotkeys need setting again.** 1.x took the re-read and re-author keys as
DirectX scan codes; 2.0 uses SkyrimNet's key picker, under the new *Hotkeys*
section. If that section is missing from the panel after upgrading, add these
lines to `SKSE\Plugins\SkyrimNet\config\plugins\SkyrimNet Relationships\settings.yaml`
while the game is closed, then set them in the panel:

```yaml
rereadKey: 0
rereadKeyModifier: None
reauthorKey: 0
reauthorKeyModifier: None
enrollKey: 0
enrollKeyModifier: None
```

### Optional: the disposition block library

If you use SeverActions, this mod ships a library of character blocks you can
import and apply to anyone.

SeverActions' import window takes **pasted JSON text**, not a file, so all you
need is the text:

1. Open [relationships_bio_blocks.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deadohiosky48/SkyrimNet-Relationships/main/library/relationships_bio_blocks.json)
   in a browser.
2. Select all and copy.
3. Paste it into SeverActions' bio block import window.
4. Apply blocks to someone.

A copy also ships in the download, as `relationships_bio_blocks.json` under
`Docs\SkyrimNet Relationships\` *inside the zip* - open it in a text editor and
copy the contents. Once installed it is at
`...\Skyrim Special Edition\Data\Docs\SkyrimNet Relationships\` with Vortex or a
manual install, or inside the mod's own folder under MO2.

> This is **not** the `docs` folder in the GitHub repository. That one holds
> developer documentation; the library lives in `library/`.

**It is optional and changes no mechanics.** Everything works without it. What
it does is make the judge more accurate. Applied blocks are the most direct
evidence this mod ever gets about someone - written deliberately about that
person rather than inferred from a bio - so they outrank the bio where the two
disagree. Without them nothing breaks; the mod simply goes on guessing from
what it can read.

Four tabs, one per axis this mod reads:

| tab | what it settles |
|---|---|
| Relationships: Attachment | how much they need to be the only one |
| Relationships: Expression | how much of what they feel they show |
| Relationships: Drawn To | who they are drawn to |
| Relationships: Limits | the line they will not cross |

Apply **one** block from Attachment, Expression and Drawn To. Each is a single
value, and when a character is authored a single block from one of these tabs is
not weighed as evidence: it is the answer. Two from the same tab is a
contradiction, so neither wins and the judge goes back to reading the bio. A
recorded marriage still outranks a Drawn To block. Limits can take several.

**Balance matters more than restraint.** Blocks describing physical taste say
nothing about how someone expresses feeling, so a character carrying twenty of
those and none of these leaves the emotional questions to be answered from
whatever else is lying around - and what is lying around is the physical.
Apply as many true blocks as you like; just make sure the emotional side is
described too.

SeverActions keeps block assignments for all of your saves at once, so a block
applied in one playthrough is applied in every other.

Blocks are read when a character is first authored, and again whenever their
disposition is reviewed - so applying one later is not wasted. It pulls them
toward it gradually instead of all at once. To have it land immediately,
re-author the character with the Re-Author Hotkey.

### Optional: the arousal block library

A second library, [arousal_bio_blocks.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deadohiosky48/SkyrimNet-Relationships/main/library/arousal_bio_blocks.json),
describes desire rather than disposition. It imports the same way and ships
beside the first one.

| tab | what it describes |
|---|---|
| Arousal: Appetite | how readily desire comes to them - apply one |
| Arousal: Stirred By | what wakes it - a few is normal |
| Arousal: Cooled By | what puts it out for a while - a few is normal |

**It unlocks nothing.** Whether someone is free to be intimate with you is still
settled by their disposition and your bond. These blocks shape whether they want
to *right now*, so the same person can say yes one evening and no the next, and
mean both. Without them someone still has good and bad nights, read from the
moment; with them, the reasons are theirs. Someone cooled by danger is not in
the mood in a crypt, and someone stirred by kindness might be after watching you
give your coin away.

**Wanting is never read as falling.** Someone stirred by exactly what you just
did has been stirred, and that is all. It can lead a casual character to bed; it
never makes anyone fall in love with you.

Keep them consistent with the rest of a character's blocks. Someone carrying
SeverActions' *Shy* or *Reserved* is more likely *Slow to Kindle* than *Easily
Stirred*.

## Configuration

Everything is tunable from SkyrimNet's settings page. A tier is 500 points and
Spouse is 2,500, and the defaults are calibrated so that nothing moves quickly.

**Bond Pace** is the setting most people will want. Slow, Normal or Fast, under
*Pacing* — roughly twice as long, as-is, or roughly half. It multiplies what is
earned in both directions, so on Slow a misstep also costs less, and it never
changes where a relationship can end up: only how long the road is. Finer knobs
sit underneath it and all of them still work; this moves them together.

Notable knobs: how much conversation can move a bond in a day, how long between
moments where a relationship is allowed to be redefined, how long someone must
have been enrolled before romance can begin at all, whether personalities are
allowed to change over time and how much must have happened first.

Under *Seeding*, **Limit Seeds by Time Known** caps a newly enrolled person's
head start by how long you have actually known each other, read from the oldest
thing they remember about you. Met today or yesterday and they start as a
stranger, however eventful the day was; a week in, an acquaintance; a month, a
friend. Longer than that, or married, and the record alone decides. Nothing else
is affected: what happens once they are enrolled is earned at the usual pace,
and the Re-Read Hotkey will lift someone later once there is more history to
read. Turn it off if you would rather a single memorable day count for
everything.

If someone's points sit just short of Lover and do not move, they are waiting
for your answer: someone who has reached the point of asking is held below the
threshold until you answer. Everything held is returned in full the moment you
say yes.

The setting that matters most to whether the mod works at all is **LLM
Variant**, which chooses the model serving every background assessment this mod
makes. See [Diary entries, and which model sees what](docs/DIARY_AND_LLM_ROUTING.md)
for what to point it at and why — and for how SkyrimNet's automatic diary
entries work, since this mod leans on them harder than on anything else.

## Status

**Released.** Enrollment, authoring, conversational scoring, the romantic gate,
disposition drift, the consent prompt and the dashboard are all built and
confirmed in live play across more than a hundred characters.

Known limitations, honestly:

- **No likes or dislikes, for now.** 2.0 removed Romantasy's, which were built on
  game statistics. They return in a later update, written in the mod's own terms
  and able to change over time.
- **A small background model can garble a sentence.** Each character's WHY and
  LIMIT are written by the model you point LLM Variant at; a small local model
  occasionally repeats a word. Re-authoring rewrites them.
- **Uninstalling does not fully reverse it.** What this mod writes lives in the
  save and its co-save. Removing the mod stops anything new from happening; it
  does not return a save to the state it was in before.

## Troubleshooting

Most repairs are in the game now: point at someone and use a hotkey, or open
their card on the dashboard and use the tools there.
The same tools are reachable through SkyrimNet's web API while the game is
running, for scripting or for someone nowhere near you: every call is a POST to
`http://127.0.0.1:8080/game-data?api=execute-quest-script-function`, and
`http://127.0.0.1:8080/game-data?api=nearby-actors` lists everyone loaded, with
their FormIDs.

**Nothing seems to be happening at all.** Check
`Data\SKSE\Plugins\SkyrimNet Relationships\logs\snrom.log`. A healthy log shows
follower sweeps, `Talk assessment sent`, and verdicts, each line stamped with
the time. If it is silent, the usual causes are that SkyrimNet cannot reach an
LLM backend, or that a model preset was applied in the SkyrimNet UI and dropped
this mod's `snrom_background` variant. The DLL keeps its own log as
`SkyrimNetRelationships.log` beside SKSE's logs, in
`My Games\Skyrim Special Edition\SKSE\`.

**"SkyrimNetRelationships.dll did not load."** Check that SKSE and Address
Library are installed for your game version, and that the DLL is in
`Data\SKSE\Plugins\`.

**"SkyrimNet is not loading Relationships' content."** 2.0 needs SkyrimNet Beta
25 or newer. On Beta 25, reinstalling this mod is the usual fix.

**My save will not load after removing Romantasy.** Install the Romantasy Stub —
see *Upgrading from 1.x*.

**Someone was enrolled who should not have been.** Open their card and use
**Remove from roster**, or by name, from anywhere:

```json
{"questEditorId":"SNRom_Quest","scriptName":"SNRom_Bridge",
 "functionName":"UnenrollByName","arguments":["Lydia"]}
```

**An authored character reads wrong.** Point at them and press the **Re-Author
Hotkey**, or use **Re-author their character** on their card. It throws away the
character on file, including everything that has drifted over a long
acquaintance, and there is no undo — the old one is written to the mod's log
first, so a mistaken press is legible afterwards. **Reach for this rather than
the re-read when the problem is the person, not the progress** — a devoted
companion who still refuses to share you, or whose stated reasons are about
someone they are not. By hand:

```json
{"questEditorId":"SNRom_Quest","scriptName":"SNRom_Bridge",
 "functionName":"ReauthorCharacter","arguments":["0x000A2C95"]}
```

**One trait is wrong and the rest are right.** Use **Correct one trait** on
their card. By hand: field 0 is intimacy (0 casual, 1 romantic, 2 guarded,
3 never), 1 is ardor (0–4), 2 is exclusivity (0–100), and all three arguments
are required:

```json
{"questEditorId":"SNRom_Quest","scriptName":"SNRom_Bridge",
 "functionName":"SetCharacterField","arguments":["0x000A2C95",2,60]}
```

**Someone was read from a bio or a journal that was wrong, and you have since
fixed it.** Fix the material first — correct the bio, write the missing diary
entries, apply the blocks — then point at them and press the **Re-Read
Hotkey**, or use **Re-read their standing** on their card. You get a
notification that the read was sent and another with the answer a few seconds
later; every refusal says why. **It can raise someone but never lower them**:
the seed is a floor, so it cannot pay twice for the same history and cannot take
anything away. By hand:

```json
{"questEditorId":"SNRom_Quest","scriptName":"SNRom_Bridge",
 "functionName":"ReseedActor","arguments":["0x000A2C95"]}
```

**A new game remembers people from an old one.** Points, tiers, and everything
this mod decides about someone belong to one playthrough, and SkyrimNet keeps
its memories per playthrough too. Two things can still make a new game look
familiar:

- **SeverActions bio blocks are shared by every save.** Blocks you applied to
  someone in one playthrough are applied in every other, and this mod reads
  them. A Drawn To, Expression or Attachment block decides that trait outright.
  Remove the block in SeverActions if it doesn't belong in this playthrough.
- **A new game started before 1.8.1 could inherit an older playthrough's
  characters**, if it was the first save to run 1.4.1 or later. Give the
  playthrough a store of its own (nothing is deleted, and `AdoptLegacyStore`
  undoes it), then re-author anyone who still looks wrong:

  ```json
  {"questEditorId":"SNRom_Quest","scriptName":"SNRom_Bridge",
   "functionName":"StartFreshStore","arguments":[]}
  ```
