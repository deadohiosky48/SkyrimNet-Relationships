1. Becoming someone they could marry

Marriage begins at the top of the ladder. Only someone whose bond with you has reached Beloved — 2,500 points — can say yes to a proposal, and only if romance between you is possible at all: who they are drawn to, and never your own kin. Below Beloved a proposal is turned aside, in their own words, however the moment comes up.

Talking about a future together, and meaning it, is one of the largest things that can happen between two lovers, so it moves the bond like one. It is often what carries them the last of the way.

One spouse at a time, unless you turn on Marry More Than One Spouse in the plugin settings. Turning it off never ends a marriage you already have; it only stops a new one.

2. The rings

Relationships adds its own rings, sold by the traders who deal in jewellery — thirteen of them across Skyrim. Three shapes are for sale, each in gold, silver or ebony; a fourth is made only at a wedding.

  • You do not need a ring to propose. But a ring given is a ring worn: whoever has yours keeps it on.
  • SeverActions outfits will not take it off. The first time one of the mod's rings meets an outfit SeverActions enforces, all of them go on SeverActions' equipment blacklist, so no outfit, lock or undress removes them. Take them off that list yourself and you are told, instead, why a ring is not being worn.
  • The wedding rings are chosen in the plan: for each of you, the slim set or the wide band, in any of the three metals. By custom a man wears the wide band and a woman the slim set; either may have either.

3. Asking

Ask in your own words

There is no special line to say. Ask them to marry you the way you would ask anyone — on a clifftop, over supper, in the middle of an argument — and they answer as themselves. Someone at Beloved may also ask you first.

When the answer is yes, a card asks you to confirm: Are the two of you promised to each other? Choose Yes, we are engaged, No, I spoke too soon, or Not yet. Nothing is recorded until you do.

Then give the ring, in conversation: say you have one for them. A card lists the rings you carry, and asks before it is handed over.

A no is an answer for now. Give it a few days before you ask again — though they may change their mind sooner, and their yes is theirs to give.

4. The planner, or none

For a planned wedding, hire a planner. Five priestesses plan weddings, one at the temple of each great city. Ask one to plan yours; her fee is 50 gold, paid when she agrees. She gives you A Wedding Planner's Book, which sets out every choice a wedding can hold. Read it.

5. Planning the wedding

Everything is settled by talking to your planner. Your betrothed can settle things too, and will have opinions. The planner leads: settle what you bring her, and she asks for what is still missing, one question at a time. A plain answer is an answer — “no guests”, “the usual ceremony”.

PartWhat to knowSaid, for instance
The dayA day and an hour: morning, noon, afternoon, evening or night. “In two days, at noon.”
The placeA place by name — a temple, a hall, an inn — or the spot you are standing on. A name nobody can find their way to needs what it is by: the nearest temple, hall, inn or town. “The Temple of Dibella.” “Right here, this spot.”
Who marries youAny priest or priestess — the planner herself included — or a hold's jarl, by hold. Whoever holds the hall on the day comes. “Hamal will marry us.” “The Jarl of Whiterun.”
GuestsAnyone you know, by name, and whose guests they are: yours, your betrothed's, or both. “My guests will be Orla and you, Senna.”
Where everyone standsMarked by standing on each spot. See below. “Hamal will stand here.”
The ceremonyVows, each “do you take” and the pronouncement always. Then four parts, each in or out: an address to everyone gathered, a reading or blessing, the rings exchanged, the kiss. “A blessing, but no speech. Rings, and a kiss.”
The ringsEach of yours: the slim set or the wide band, in gold, silver or ebony. “Gold for me, silver for her.”
What they wearYour betrothed chooses, and with SeverActions keeps what they are wearing when they do, and changes into it on the day. Give them something new first, if you like. What you wear is up to you. “I'll wear this.”

Where everyone stands: go and stand there

This is the part nobody would guess. A spot is marked exactly where you are standing when you say it — there is no pointing, and no “over there”. So go to the place before the day, with your planner (ask her to come with you) or your betrothed, and for each spot, walk onto it and say who stands there:

  1. Where the officiant stands. “Hamal will stand here.”
  2. Where you stand. “I will stand here.”
  3. Where your betrothed stands. “Daighre will stand here.” Unmarked, they stand beside you.
  4. Where each side's guests gather, if you have guests on that side. “Daighre's guests will gather here.” “My guests here.”

One spot each time you say it; say it again from somewhere else to move it. Or leave it to the planner: “Wherever you think best.”

Worth knowing

  • “Right here” means where you stand. Choosing the place by standing on it works anywhere, even a crag in the middle of nowhere; everyone is walked to it on the day.
  • The plan is on your betrothed's card in the dashboard: what is settled, what is still needed before the day, and what is still to talk through.
  • Change anything, any time before the day, the same way: say so. Guests added or taken off, the day moved, the rings chosen again. There is no shame in moving a wedding — tell the planner before the day comes.

If something was not written down

  • A model decides what was agreed, and once in a while it misses one. Check the plan on your betrothed's card after a planning talk.
  • Fix it by hand. Change the plan by hand on that card sets any part — the day, the place or right here, who marries you, guests and their sides, where someone stands, the ceremony, the rings, the clothes — exactly as if it had been agreed.

6. The day

A few hours before, you are told: Your wedding to them is today. Your betrothed changes into the clothes they chose, and everyone coming sets out, each in their own time, for the place and their mark. Be at the place at the hour, standing with your betrothed, near the one marrying you, and the ceremony begins. Anyone not there by then is brought in to join the others.

You are waited for, but not forever. Six hours after the hour, a wedding you never came to is missed, and your betrothed feels it: it costs the bond, and whether the engagement survives is theirs to say. The rest of the plan is kept for another day. If the day comes with the plan unfinished, it is simply put off, with no blame.

  1. If chosen

    The address. The officiant speaks to everyone gathered.

  2. If chosen

    A reading or blessing.

  3. Your turn

    Your vows. A notice tells you: look at your betrothed, and speak your vows — in your own words, typed or spoken.

  4. Theirs

    Their vows, in theirs.

  5. Your turn

    “Do you take…?” Look at the officiant, and answer plainly: “I do.”

  6. Theirs

    They are asked the same, and answer.

  7. If chosen

    The rings. Exchanged at their step; nothing to do but be there. Each of you comes away wearing one.

  8. Always

    The pronouncement. You are married.

  9. If chosen

    The kiss. With a mod that plays kisses for SkyrimNet, it can be a scene of its own; the ceremony waits for it to end.

  10. After

    You are presented, the guests applaud, and they stay about two hours to celebrate and wish you well.

Look at whoever you are answering. SkyrimNet gives your words to the person under your crosshair. Facing your betrothed, what you say is your vows; facing the officiant, it is your answer. The notices on screen tell you whom to look at.

The day is kept: your wedding day and every anniversary are on the dashboard's calendar, beside birthdays and Skyrim's festivals, and each person's card shows their family.
