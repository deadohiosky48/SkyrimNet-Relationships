SkyrimNet Relationships · preparing your save

Give It Something To Read

This mod does not invent your companions. It reads what your game already says about them — and the quality of what it says is most of the quality of what you get back.

The one thing worth understanding

When a companion comes under observation, the mod asks a question: where do these two actually stand? It answers by reading the record your playthrough has produced — the bio, the journal, what she remembers. Then it writes down a starting point instead of beginning everyone at zero.

So a follower you have travelled with for eighty hours can still read as a stranger, if nothing in your game ever wrote down that anything happened. The mod is not guessing badly. It is reading an empty page.

Which means the useful work is not in the mod's settings. It is in making sure your game has something to say.

What it reads, best first

Not all sources are equally useful, and the difference is not subtle. The good ones describe what happened between two people. The weak ones report a number that could mean anything.

SourceWorthWhy
Dynamic bios Best A living description of who someone is now, rewritten as you play. When a capable model keeps these updated, they say things like “she manages his household and is the mother of his children” — a fact about the relationship, in plain words. Nothing else comes close.
Diary entries Best Her own private account of what happened, in her voice. The most reliable thing available, because it records events rather than impressions — and it accumulates on its own as you play.
Bio blocks
(SeverActions)		 Best The only source you write deliberately. A block you apply is treated as a direct statement and outranks everything else about that one trait. Optional, and the strongest tool you have for a character who is being read wrong.
Memories Good What she recalls of specific moments. Useful and specific, but fragmentary — a handful of scenes rather than a picture of the whole.
Static bio Good Whoever the character was before you met. Real information, but about her rather than about the two of you, and it never changes.
Relationship rank Weak A single number the game sets, mostly to the same value for everyone you recruit. It cannot tell you why, and on a typical save it is the same for a housecarl and a lover.
Follower rapport
(SeverActions)		 Weak Can be switched off entirely, and often sits at its starting value even when it is on. Used only as a floor — it can raise someone, never lower them.

Why prose beats numbers: “rank 3” is true of almost everyone. “He knelt on the stone floor of the Temple of Mara and asked her to marry him” can only be true of one pair of people. The second one is what the mod is looking for.

Setting it up

Step one

Turn dynamic bios on

In SkyrimNet's settings. This is the single highest-value thing you can do. Without it, every companion is described by a bio written before you ever met them.

Step two

Use a good model for them

Bio updates are worth spending your best model on, even if you run a smaller local one for ordinary chat. A capable model writes bios that need almost no correction; a weak one invents things you will have to undo.

Step three

Let the journal fill up

Diary entries accumulate by themselves as you play — nothing to configure. But they do not accumulate reliably, so learn to write one on demand when a moment deserves it. That is the next section, and it matters.

Optional

Apply bio blocks

If you use SeverActions, the mod ships a library of blocks describing how someone loves — how much they need to be the only one, how openly they show feeling, who they are drawn to. Import it and apply what fits; instructions are two sections down.

You do not have to wait for it

Diaries and bios update on their own during ordinary play, but not reliably, and never on cue. The moment that mattered most is exactly the one that can go unrecorded — and if it was never written down, nothing can read it. You can trigger both by hand, for any character, at any time.

In the SkyrimNet UI, open the character, and use Write Diary and Update Bio. Do not hesitate to use them. If you look after something significant and see that neither has been touched, that is the moment to press the buttons yourself.

Diary first, then bio — always in that order. The dynamic bio is written from the diary. Update the bio before the entry exists and it just rewrites itself out of the old material, and the thing you were trying to capture is still missing. If you are triggering both, write the diary entry first and update the bio second.

When it is worth doing: after anything you would call a turning point — a confession, a rescue, a betrayal, a night that changed things, a death you both stood over. Check whether the diary picked it up. If it did not, write it, then update the bio. Half a dozen of these across a playthrough will do more for how your companions are read than any setting in this mod.

Getting the block library into SeverActions

This is the step that confuses people, so plainly: SeverActions' import window takes pasted JSON text, not a file. You are not browsing to the library — you are opening it, copying the text out, and pasting it in.

  1. Open the library. Easiest is straight from the project: relationships_bio_blocks.json in a browser. A copy also ships in the download as relationships_bio_blocks.json under Docs\SkyrimNet Relationships\ — open it in a text editor. Once installed that is under Data\Docs\SkyrimNet Relationships\ with Vortex or a manual install, or inside the mod's own folder under MO2.
  2. Select all and copy. The entire contents, brackets included.
  3. Paste it into SeverActions' bio block import window and import. It adds four tabs: Attachment, Expression, Drawn To and Limits.
  4. Apply blocks to a companion. One each from Attachment, Expression and Drawn To — each is a single value, so two at once is a contradiction rather than a preference stated twice. Limits can take several.

Not the repo's docs folder. That one holds these pages. The library lives in library/. Full notes on what each tab settles, and why balance matters more than restraint, are on the project page.

If you are installing mid-playthrough

This is the case that rewards preparation, because your existing followers already have history and the mod reads them once — a snapshot of whatever the record says at that moment.

  1. Turn on dynamic bios and point them at a capable model before installing, not after.
  2. Let your followers' bios regenerate. Talk to each one, travel a little, give the bios a chance to be rewritten with the relationship in them. This is the step that makes the difference.
  3. Read a few of them. If a bio describes the relationship you actually have, you are ready. If it still reads like a stranger's dossier, wait a bit longer.
  4. Then install. Each follower is read as they come under observation, and a starting point is written down.

How much is enough? A companion with an accurate bio and a handful of diary entries will read well. One with an untouched bio and an empty journal will read as an acquaintance no matter how long you have travelled together — and that is honest, because nothing in your save disagrees.

What goes wrong, and what to do

Worth doing

  • Write the diary entry after the moment that mattered rather than hoping it was caught. Then update the bio — in that order.
  • Fix a bio that is simply wrong. They are editable. A sentence that misdescribes the relationship will be believed, so correcting it is the highest leverage edit available.
  • Add a bio block for a trait being read wrong. Faster and more reliable than rewriting prose and hoping.
  • Let it surprise you. The point is that each companion reacts differently. Curating every character until they all behave as expected removes the thing you installed this for.

Common traps

  • An inflated bio gets believed. A weak model may write that someone is your betrothed after a single evening. The mod now checks claims like that against what your marriage system actually recorded — but an accurate bio is still better than a corrected one.
  • Intensity is not the same as commitment. A companion who longs for you is not the same as two people who are together, and it will be read that way. That is deliberate.
  • Installing before any of this. Nothing breaks, and nothing is lost — but everyone starts lower than they deserve.

Checking your work

You do not have to guess whether it worked. When a companion is read, the mod records the standing it chose and the reason it chose it — the specific thing in your save that decided it.

What a good read looks like: “…her diary records that he knelt on the stone floor of the Temple of Mara to ask her to marry him, and she shares his hearth as the mother of his children.”

And a thin one: “…I agreed to travel with him and intend to keep my word.”

The second is not a mistake. It is an accurate reading of a record that contains very little. If the reasons look thin, the material is thin — and that is the thing to improve, rather than the mod's settings.

If it read someone wrong and you have since fixed it

This is the common shape of the problem, and it has an answer. A companion is read from a record that was thin or wrong; you go and improve the record; the standing already written down does not move on its own. You can ask for a re-read, and it is one keypress.

  1. Fix the material first. Correct the bio, write the missing diary entries, apply the blocks. The re-read only ever sees what is there at the moment you press the key, so there is no point asking before the work is done.
  2. Have them travelling with you, and put them under your crosshair. They must be an active companion — a follower you have dismissed cannot be re-read, because the scoreboard underneath only tracks people currently with you. Point at anyone else and it will tell you why rather than doing nothing.
  3. Press Home. No menu, no console, nothing to look up. You will see Re-reading the record for… straight away, and the verdict a few seconds later.

There are two answers worth recognising, and both of them are the system working. Either the companion moves — read as something higher than the record previously supported, and topped up to it — or you are told they already stand at or above what the record justifies, and nothing is added. The second is not a failed keypress. It is the mod telling you the standing it already had was right.

It can raise someone, but it can never lower them. A re-read tops a companion up to what their history justifies and subtracts whatever they already hold, so it cannot pay twice and cannot take anything away. Which means it fixes an under-read completely, and cannot walk back a read that was too generous. That asymmetry is the real reason to get the material right before the mod first reads someone rather than after.

Ask as often as you like. It is not a limited resource and it cannot compound — asking twice in a row gives the same answer the second time, because it is reading the same record. A companion whose material has not changed will simply keep reading the same way.

The key lives under Seeding in the mod's settings, as a DirectX scan code rather than a key you press to capture: 199 is Home, and 197 Pause, 211 Delete and 87 F11 are usually free if something in your load order has already claimed Home. Change the number rather than adding a modifier if it collides — a held modifier is only checked by this mod, so the other mod still fires on the base key regardless. Set it to 0 to turn the key off entirely.

Everything the key does is also reachable through SkyrimNet's web API if you would rather script it — see Troubleshooting on the project page.
